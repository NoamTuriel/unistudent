How setup works

Setup is where UniStudent reads your whole course. It takes a while, you do it once per course, and it's the reason every answer afterwards can point to the exact page or lecture minute.

The course Wiki, in plain words

Your course files are written for people: long PDFs, slides with half sentences, three-hour recordings. Your AI can't reread all of that every time you ask a question, and if it skims, it fills the gaps from the internet.

So during setup, the AI reads everything once and writes a set of notes for itself: the course Wiki. Some people call this an LLM wiki, meaning a wiki that an AI writes for an AI to read. When you ask a question later, your AI looks it up in the Wiki first, and the Wiki remembers which file and which page every fact came from.

The Wiki sits in a hidden folder inside your course folder. You never need to open it or edit it. What you see are your own files, and the study packs made from the Wiki.

What happens during setup

You start it by saying "Set up my course with UniStudent". It asks one question at a time and says before each step whether it will take a while.

  1. A few questions

    Your university, your course, and where your files are.

  2. Gathering your files

    From the folder you point it to, from the course inbox, or, with a plugin for your university, straight from the course site. Downloading can be slow, and it tells you so.

  3. Choosing what to do with recordings

    Skip them, only download them, or decide one recording at a time. Analyzing video frames is a separate yes or no.

  4. Reading everything and writing the Wiki

    The long part. It reads each file and writes the Wiki pages for every subject.

  5. What's next

    It explains what it built, how to ask questions, how to add new files, and how to make your first study pack.

You can stop at any point. Running setup again picks up where you left off.

The more you give it, the better it knows your course

It only knows what's in the Wiki. When something isn't there, it has to answer from general knowledge, and it marks that answer with ⚠️ so you know.

Choosing what to turn on

Transcribe your recordings?

Usually yes. A transcript is what lets it cite the exact minute, write down the lecturer's announcements, and catch every "this will be on the exam". Setup offers three options:

  • Skip recordings for now
  • Only download them
  • Decide one recording at a time

Before anything starts, it shows how many recordings you have, how many hours of audio, and how long it expects on your computer.

Analyze video frames too?

Say yes when your lecturer teaches from slides or draws on the board: graphs, formulas, diagrams. That's common in economics, maths and science.

Skip it when the lectures are mostly talking. The transcripts alone are already useful, and you save the most AI usage and time.

It's a setting for each course, so you can change your mind later.

You do this once per course

A big course with hours of recordings can take a long time to set up. Let it run, take a break, and come back. You can stop whenever you want, and it continues from where it stopped.

After setup, new files don't start anything over. Drop them in the course's inbox folder and say Add my new files. It reads only the new ones, adds them to the Wiki, and tells you which study packs changed.
